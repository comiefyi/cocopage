Solar Sponge VPP

A Virtual Power Plant that turns Manhattan's commercial buildings into intelligent energy sponges — absorbing excess solar during the day and dispatching power back to the grid at peak demand.

Event Grid Tech Hackathon
Focus area NYC Grid Modernization
Solution type Software + VPP Platform
The Problem

NYC's grid wasn't built for solar.

New York City's electrical grid faces a growing challenge: as rooftop and utility-scale solar capacity expands, midday generation increasingly outpaces demand — creating curtailment events where clean energy is wasted. Meanwhile, evening peak demand spikes stress aging transmission infrastructure, driving up costs and emissions.

⚡ The fundamental mismatch: solar peaks at noon, electricity demand peaks at 6–9 PM. Without flexible demand or storage, surplus clean energy is lost and fossil peakers fill the gap.

Traditional battery storage is expensive and limited in scale. We asked: what if Manhattan's vast stock of commercial buildings — data centers, warehouses, and factories — could collectively act as the city's battery?

Our Solution

Solar Sponge VPP

Solar Sponge is a software-driven Virtual Power Plant that aggregates flexible load across commercial buildings in Manhattan. By coordinating energy absorption and dispatch in real time, it bridges the gap between solar generation and grid demand.

How It Works

Absorb. Store. Dispatch.

1

Monitor solar surplus

The platform tracks real-time solar generation across NYC's grid zones, detecting when supply is outpacing demand and clean energy would otherwise be curtailed.

2

Shift flexible loads in commercial buildings

Enrolled data centers, warehouses, and factories receive signals to pull in extra energy during surplus windows — pre-cooling systems, charging backup power, running intensive compute jobs — effectively "soaking up" the excess solar.

3

Dispatch stored energy at peak demand

When evening demand spikes, buildings reduce their draw from the grid and release stored thermal or electrical energy back, acting as distributed generation assets in a coordinated Virtual Power Plant.

4

Revenue sharing and incentives

Participating buildings earn revenue through demand-response payments and capacity market credits, creating a business model that aligns commercial interests with grid stability.
